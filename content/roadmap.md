# Roadmap

Janus is being developed incrementally, with each stage of the antimatter pipeline validated independently before being integrated into larger end-to-end studies. This validation-first philosophy ensures that future optimization and application studies are built upon physically reliable foundations.

---

# Current Status

The current release includes:

* Geant4-based antiproton production
* Deterministic charged-particle transport
* Relativistic Boris particle integration
* Magnetic horn, drift, dipole, and quadrupole elements
* Hierarchical transport validation
* Beam diagnostics and first-order optics benchmarking
* 3D beam visualization

These components provide a validated computational foundation for future development.

---

# Stage 1 — Transport Physics

The next phase expands the transport pipeline toward a more realistic representation of antimatter beamlines.

Planned additions include:

* Solenoids
* Sextupole magnets
* Higher-order magnetic elements
* Field-map support
* Material interactions during transport
* Beamline aperture modelling

---

# Stage 2 — Beam Conditioning

Following validated transport, Janus will model the processes required to prepare antiprotons for long-term confinement.

Planned additions include:

* Momentum deceleration
* Electron cooling
* Stochastic cooling
* Beam matching
* Trap injection studies

---

# Stage 3 — Storage

The transport framework will be extended toward antimatter storage systems.

Planned additions include:

* Penning trap modelling
* Magnetic confinement
* Storage lifetime studies
* Antiproton accumulation
* Antihydrogen formation

---

# Stage 4 — Optimization

Once the complete computational pipeline has been validated, optimization studies will be introduced.

Research directions include:

* Antiproton production optimization
* Magnetic horn optimization
* Beam transport optimization
* Beam optics optimization
* End-to-end pipeline optimization
* Multi-objective design studies
* Sensitivity and uncertainty analysis

---

# Stage 5 — Applications

The long-term objective of Janus is to serve as a computational platform for studying practical antimatter systems.

Potential applications include:

* Accelerator physics
* Antimatter storage systems
* Medical physics
* Radiation transport
* Antimatter-assisted propulsion
* Digital twins of antimatter production facilities

---

# Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision of Janus is a modular, validation-driven computational framework capable of modeling the complete antimatter pipeline—from particle production through transport, conditioning, storage, optimization, and application.

Rather than emphasizing a single simulation stage, Janus aims to provide a unified platform where each physical subsystem is independently validated before being integrated into larger computational studies.
